Pace Mismatch Test

When a fast team plays a slow one, does the game come in slower than the average of the two paces, so totals built that way go under? Registered in PREREGISTRATION.md before the run; regular-season games only.

1 · Mechanism: possessions, no line involved

Regression of the game's actual pace on the average of the two rolling paces and the absolute gap. The idea needs a negative gap coefficient; the registered rule needs it negative in both periods and significant in confirmation.

Gap coefficient on possessions, by sample

Possessions per 48 minutes per pace point of gap, 95% intervals from resampling game days. Below zero would support the idea.

The coefficient is zero in every cut: with season dummies, regulation-only games, 10- and 30-game windows, and the six earlier seasons (which need no odds). The confirmation interval ( to ) rules out a gap effect more negative than possessions per pace point, about −0.14 points of total per point of gap at roughly 2.2 points per possession. The smallest effect this sample could detect at 80% power is possessions per pace point.

The average-pace coefficient is above 1 ( in confirmation). That is what you get when each team's style adds to the game: a matchup of two fast teams is faster than the average of their paces implies, and two slow teams slower. It is my reading of the number, not a registered test.

Exploratory lead: average pace level

In the same regressions, the average pace of the two teams predicts r positively and the model's residual too (confirmation: r , model residual , interval to ), which means both the market total and the model under-weight how fast the two teams play. It is positive with season dummies and without, but it is positive only weakly in discovery (, interval to ), and nobody registered it, so it is a lead to test rather than a finding. E05 tested pace-normalised team statistics and rejected them; a pace-level feature in the totals model was not part of that.

Limits

  • The gap is measured from 20-game averages, which are noisy; the window checks (10 and 30 games) give the same null, and the interval is tight enough that a real effect of the size the theory suggests would have shown.
  • Games with fewer than 10 prior games for either team are excluded, so early-season matchups are not tested.
  • The market and model tests are weaker than the mechanism test (the market total carries about 18 points of noise per game).
  • Holm across the 8 angles is pending; this angle's input is 1.0. The feature test was not run because the gate failed.
